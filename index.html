What happens when the easiest thing to build isn't the hardest problem to solve?

The Smart Stack became a team productivity platform across India and the US. The interesting part wasn't the repository. It was deciding what belonged in it, removing a dependency that slowed learning, and making adoption part of the product.

01 / V1

What didn't work

The first version combined Python tools, saved prompts and LLM use cases such as BRD generation. AI integration introduced production tollgates, while team usage was limited.

02 / DECISION

Remove AI from scope

The goal shifted from "use AI" to "experiment fast, fail fast, learn fast." A central utility platform could provide immediate value without waiting on AI integration.

03 / PRIORITIZATION

Decide what gets built

Tool intake considered time spent, monthly usage, potential time savings and the number of people impacted.

04 / ADOPTION

Make usage part of the product

Tools were integrated into Smart Stack, feedback was collected directly, and new releases were demonstrated through Teams meetings and monthly release notes.

05 / SCALE

Make contribution easier

A simple open contribution model, README guidance and coding standards helped make the platform something the wider team could contribute to.

06 / ACCESS

Remove setup friction

A shared-drive batch setup process installed libraries, WebDriver and ChromeDriver so users didn't have to be Python-environment experts.

IDENTIFY→EVALUATE→BUILD→RELEASE→FEEDBACK→IMPROVE

PharmEasy Chronic Care Teardown.

Product concept · Retention · Medication management

THE QUESTION

Can medication management become a retention mechanism?

THE PROPOSAL

An integrated reminder and stock-management experience that makes recurring medication easier to manage and reorder.

01 / PROBLEM

Recurring medication creates recurring friction.

Chronic patients need to manage doses, track remaining medication and remember when to reorder. That friction can contribute to churn after the first purchase.

02 / DECISION

Prioritize utility over a social dashboard.

Three potential solutions were considered. The Reminder & Stock Management System was prioritized because the "utility gap" was a higher-impact, lower-effort lever than a Family Track portal.

03 / CONCEPT

Reminder Tab.

Daily dosage automatically deducts stock. When less than 10 days of medication remain, the system triggers a prominent restock alert.

04 / MEASUREMENT

Measure the habit loop.

If shipped, I'd track notification click-through rate and user churn after the "Reminder to Buy" alert is sent.

TAKE MEDICATION→STOCK DECREASES→<10 DAYS→REMINDER→REORDER
